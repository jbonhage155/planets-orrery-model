Box: cutting and the screen opening

The walls and lid, in the order that keeps everything reachable, how to cut straight with the Dremel, and a full-size template for the screen opening. All sizes in inches.

Redesigned 11 Nov 2026: this page is out of date for the walls and lid. The ceiling is now 6¾ in up, the front is one sloped panel (the 8⅜ in piece with the screen opening), and the ceiling slides out the back round the column. Templates for the sides, ceiling and back door: Panel templates. The Dremel how-to (section 3) and gluing notes (section 6) still apply.

1. Order of work

  1. Measure the screen (section 4) and print the template (section 5).
  2. Practise. A few cuts in acrylic scrap (the 11/16 in strip, the cracked plate), then one 2 in cut near a corner of the spare polycarbonate sheet. This finds the dial setting and the guide offset (section 3).
  3. The two sides (⅛ in acrylic), and their holes while they lie flat.
  4. Tape the sides to the base and measure the gap between them. The plan says 11.75; cut the front, the slope and the lid to what you measure.
  5. The polycarbonate sheet: the screen opening first, while the sheet is whole, then the two strips.
  6. The lid (⅛ in polycarbonate) and its 13/32 in column hole.
  7. Dry-fit everything with masking tape, the screen set in its opening (backing paper still on its tape). Check the tubes still turn with the lid on.
  8. Glue the walls: sides, front, slope. The back stays open: it is the lift-off door.
  9. Screen, Pi and electronics in, through the open top. Run it.
  10. The lid goes on last, then the arms. From the back door you can reach the servos, but not the screen or the front of the box: the servo ring is in the way.
Don't cement the lid yet. Tape it down until the model has run for a few days. The 7/16 in collar goes on Neptune's tube under the lid before the lid goes on, and the arm collars clamp on after it, so the lid can only come off again with the arms removed.

3. Straight cuts with the Dremel

Dremel 3100, the 565 cutting guide screwed onto its nose, the 561 multipurpose bit (the ⅛ in spiral). It cuts the polycarbonate and the acrylic. Nothing is snapped, so nothing can run off the line.

  1. Board underneath. Lay the whole sheet flat on scrap plywood, foam board or thick cardboard, so both sides of the cut are held up. The bit cuts a groove into the board. Set the guide so the bit sticks out about 1/16–⅛ in past the sheet.
  2. Clamp a straightedge (steel ruler or a straight board) at both ends, through the sheet to the table. The guide's foot slides along it. Keep the same side of the foot against it for the whole cut and don't twist the tool.
  3. Find the guide offset once. On scrap, cut along the straightedge, then measure from the straightedge to the nearest edge of the slot. That number is the offset: enter it in section 4 and the template prints where the straightedge goes for every cut.
  4. The bit is ⅛ in wide. The line is the edge of the piece you keep; the whole slot goes on the waste side.
  5. Speed: Dremel gives 15,000–20,000 rpm for this bit in plastic, about 4 to 6 on the dial. Start at 4 on scrap. Chips should fly off like snow. Stringy, melted chips or a slot that welds shut behind the bit: move along faster, or go down one number. Chatter or grabbing: up one number.
  6. Which way to push: with the straightedge on your left, push the tool away from you. The bit then pulls the tool against the straightedge. The other way it pushes off and wanders. The template's arrows show it for each cut.
  7. One steady pass. Start with the bit off the edge of the sheet, tool running, both hands on it. Don't stop in the middle: where the bit sits still, the plastic melts. For the screen opening, drill a ¼ in hole inside each corner first (they are on the template): each cut starts in one hole and ends in the next.
  8. Hold the offcut. Tape across the slot behind the bit every few inches, so the offcut can't drop, pinch the bit or break off the last inch. Slow down for the last inch.
  9. Clean up. The melted lip snaps off with a fingernail or the back of a knife. Then a few strokes with 220 sandpaper on a flat block. Leave the film on until the panel is glued.
Acrylic is the brittle one. Keep the guide flat and never lever the tool in the slot. Polycarbonate will not crack, but it cuts gummier (stringy chips, a bigger burr). Both melt where the bit sits still, acrylic at the lower temperature: keep moving.

Why the 12 in snap went wrong: a score has to be about a third of the way through and the same depth from end to end, and the bend has to come down evenly along the whole line at once. A shallow stretch, or pressing at one end, and the crack leaves the line, as yours did halfway. Cast acrylic (these sheets) is the least forgiving. Keep scoring for short cuts only.

Safety glasses for everyone nearby: the chips are hot. The tool is loud, so the kids watch from a step back.

4. Measure the screen

The screen goes in from the front (9 Nov 2026). Its glass has a strip of 3M tape round the back of its edge, and the metal tray behind it is smaller than the glass. So the hole is the size of the tray: the tray and the Pi drop through, and the glass lip sticks to the outside of the panel and hides the cut edge. Measured from the back (user, 9 Nov): glass 7⅝ × 4⅜, lip 9/16 at the left end and 7/16 at the right (seen from the front), ¼ at the top, ⅛ at the bottom; the lit picture is 6 1/16 × 3⅜. Fractions like 7/16 or 4 3/8 can be typed as they are.

Check which end is which: "left" and "top" here are as seen from the front, with the picture the right way up.

The glass is centred on the panel, which is how the 3D model's fit check has it (the Pi clears Saturn's servo by ¼ in). The opening follows the tray (or the picture), so it sits a little off-centre. Cut the hole a touch small and file it until the tray just drops in: the bottom lip is only ⅛ in, so there is little to spare there.

5. Screen opening template, full size

Landscape, printed at 100 % ("actual size"): check the 2 in bar, and that the TOP EDGE and BOTTOM lines are 5 in apart. Trim the paper along the TOP EDGE and LEFT END lines. Lay it on the whole polycarbonate sheet, film still on, with those two trimmed edges flush on two factory edges at a corner, and stick it down flat (glue stick, or tape all round). That face becomes the outside of the box. Cut straight through the paper.

Blue lines: where the straightedge's edge goes for each cut; the shaded side is the straightedge itself, and the arrow is the way to push. They appear once the guide offset is typed in above. Cut in the order ① ② ③ ④: for the first two the straightedge lies on the waste, which is still held firmly then. Tape over each finished slot so the waste can't shift.

6. Gluing

  • Test first on offcuts: acrylic to acrylic, and polycarbonate to acrylic. Acrylic cement doesn't always hold polycarbonate. If the test joint peels, use 5-minute epoxy for the front and the slope.
  • Sides onto the base, flush with its edges, taped square. Run the cement along the joint from the needle; it wicks in. Outdoors or at an open window.
  • Front wall between the sides, on the base. Slope between the sides: its two ends are the glue joints. Its long edges only touch the front wall and the lid along a line; leave them, no bevels needed.
  • Floor parts first: anything that needs a hole in the base near the front (the driver board's standoffs) is easier before the front wall is on. Velcro works too.
  • Magnet blocks and the door as in the build checklist, phase 7. The blocks: ¾ in squares from the unused ¼ in acrylic sheet, or two ⅛ in scraps glued together.
  • Lid last, taped, after the screen and the electronics are in and running.
