Planet rings

A full-size template for Saturn's, Uranus's and Neptune's rings in 2 mm craft foam, and two tilt gauges for setting them. All sizes in inches.

1. Print the template

Set to the balls as measured with calipers (14 Nov 2026): Saturn 1⅞ in, Uranus 1 9/16 in; Neptune assumed the same as Uranus, so measure it if you make its ring. Each hole is cut 1/16 in smaller than its ball, so the foam grips.

In the print dialog choose Actual size or Scale 100 %, not "Fit to page". Then check the 2 in bar at the bottom with a ruler. If it isn't exactly 2 in, the rings will be off: fix the setting and reprint.

2. Cut the rings

  1. Cut each paper ring out on its outside circle, then cut out its hole: fold it gently across the middle to snip a start, then cut round the inner circle.
  2. Lay the paper ring on the foam (the back of the sheet, so marks don't show) and trace both circles with a pen, or tape it down and cut through paper and foam together.
  3. Cut the outside with sharp scissors. Start the hole with a craft-knife slit, then cut round with scissors. Slow, short snips give a smooth edge.
  4. Uranus and Neptune: cut straight across the band at the CUT mark. The ring becomes a C that wraps round the ball, and the gap goes at the bottom, round the upright rod.

Pick the foam colour rather than painting: tan or yellow for Saturn, pale blue, grey or white for Uranus and Neptune. Acrylic paint works on the foam if you want Saturn's white circles.

3. Glue them on

  1. Saturn: slide the ring over the ball to its middle; the foam stretches a little. No need to cut the ball in half.
  2. Uranus and Neptune: open the C, wrap it round the ball's middle, and close it with the gap at the bottom.
  3. Glue with white or tacky glue, or epoxy. Never super glue (Gorilla gel): it melts foam. A thin bead where the ring meets the ball, top and bottom. A few straight pins through the ring into the ball hold it while it dries.
  4. Let it dry on the bench. Then push the ball onto its upright and turn it so the ring faces the right way (below) before gluing the ball on.

4. Which way each ring faces

A planet keeps its direction relative to its arm all the way round, so set each ring once, when the ball is glued onto its upright (arms page, section 5).

  • Saturn, 25°: tilt it about the direction Saturn travels. The edge toward the Sun goes up and the outer edge down, and the front and back edges stay level. Looking along the orbit, hold the 25° gauge's bottom edge along the arm: the ring lies along the slope. With the outer edge down, people around the model see the top of the ring.
  • Uranus, upright: the ring's face looks along the arm toward the Sun, and the ring stands across the arm, like a wheel rolling sideways along the orbit (Uranus spins on its side). Check it's upright with the steel square.
  • Neptune, 28°: like Saturn, with the 28° gauge.

Before the glue sets, turn each ringed planet by hand past its neighbours. The closest spot, Saturn's ring to Uranus, should be about ⅝ in.
