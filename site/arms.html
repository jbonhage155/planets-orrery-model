Arms & collars

Each planet's arm: a clamp collar on its brass tube, a straight 3/32 in rod out to an aquarium elbow, and a short upright to the planet. All sizes in inches.

2. How long to cut each arm rod

Cut length = orbit radius − how far the rod's inner end sits from the column's centre − the elbow offset. Where the rod goes in as deep as the collar wall, it touches the brass tube. Where the wall is thicker, it stops short, and the epoxy holds it.

Elbow offset: push a rod into one leg of an elbow as far as it goes, and measure from its end to the centre line of the other leg. Pick that above and the table updates. ⅛ until it's measured.

3. Gluing the rod in straight

Sideways play doesn't matter: once the epoxy cures, LINE UP calibration (below) takes whatever direction the arm points. The angle that matters is up and down, for the gaps between arms.

  1. Collar flat on the table, on wax paper or a plastic bag. That makes its hole straight up and the arm hole dead level.
  2. Plug its hole with a scrap of the same-size tube (or a dowel) wrapped in tape. The arm end butts against it as it will against the real tube, and no epoxy gets in the hole. Snug the clamp screw on the plug.
  3. Scuff the last ½ in of the rod with sandpaper and wipe it with alcohol.
  4. Prop the far end level: on coins, washers or card, the same height as the middle of the arm hole (about 3/16 in). Gravity holds it square while it sets.
  5. Epoxy the end, push it in, and leave a small fillet round the rod at the collar.
  6. Keep epoxy out of the split and off the clamp screw, or the collar won't tighten.
  7. Cure: 5-minute epoxy handles in an hour; give it 24 hours before a planet hangs on it.

Check: collar clamped on its tube, look from the side at eye level and turn the planet once. Same height all the way round = straight. If it rises and falls, the collar is tilted on the tube (most likely Mars, Saturn or Neptune, the loose ones): re-seat it and tighten evenly before the final epoxy.

Onto the tube: clamp only, no epoxy (decided 9 Nov). Nothing can glue two tubes together, the arms come off for transport, and a grabbed planet slips instead of straining its servo. Clamp at any angle: LINE UP sets the direction.

  • Loose collars (Mars, Saturn, Neptune): one wrap of electrical tape on the tube under the collar. Butt the ends (an overlap tilts the collar), and keep it inside the collar's ⅜ in band, never down where the next tube out ends (Neptune: above the lid). If one of these arms starts to slip, snug its screw again.
  • Snug the screw, don't crank it: aluminium threads strip, and too tight squashes the thin tube onto the one inside. After each collar, check the tube inside it still turns freely.

4. Uranus and Neptune collar heights

Neptune's tube top is at 8¼ in (washers under it, 8 Nov), so its top is only about ¼ in below Uranus's. With both collars at their tube tops the two arms would be crowded. Nothing is outside Neptune's tube, so its collar doesn't have to be at the top:

  • Only Uranus's hole is drilled off-centre (Neptune's collar sits low, but its hole is in the middle like the rest). Its collar sticks up past its own tube top by about ⅛ in, and above that it wraps Saturn's tube, so drill its hole a little low to keep the rod and its glue against Uranus's tube.
  • How low: measure g, Neptune's tube top to Uranus's tube top. The hole's centre goes at most g − ⅛ in above the collar's bottom edge: g ¼ → ⅛ in; g about 9/32 → 5/32 in; g 5/16 or more → the middle (3/16 in).
  • Saturn's tube passes up through that overhang with only about 1/64 in to spare: keep epoxy out of the collar's bore when gluing the rod (plug it), and clamp only, no glue on the tube.

Keep the lid: it holds the top of the column (the stack is held at the post's foot and the lid's hole only), and it keeps fingers and dust out of the gears.

5. Gaps between the planets

Edge to edge, every pair is about 1 1/16 in apart (laid out on the floor, 24 Oct). Rings take some of that:

NeighboursBall to ballWith rings

Neighbours come closest when they line up on the same side of the Sun, so what counts is how far each ring reaches toward the Sun and away from it. A planet keeps its direction relative to its arm all the way round, so set each ring when you glue it:

  • Saturn (3 in, 25°) and Neptune (2¼ in, 28°): tilt toward the Sun, the low edge pointing in along the arm. The tilt pulls the reach in: Saturn about 1⅜ in from its centre, Neptune about 1 in.
  • Uranus (nearly upright): ring facing the Sun, like a wheel rolling sideways along its orbit. Edge-on to the Sun it would reach the full 1⅛ in both ways.

Cutting and gluing the foam rings, with a printable full-size template and tilt gauges: rings page. Set that way, the tightest spot is about ⅝ in (Saturn's ring to Uranus). The planets all sit at one height and never touch.

6. Travel mode (the home position)

On the touchscreen's settings tab (gear icon). Do this after the epoxy has fully cured.

  1. In AUTO, tap TRAVEL MODE (called LINE UP until 13 Nov 2026). Every servo turns, forward only, to the home position: every arm pointing away from the screen, the planets in one row. This is the position for clamping the arms on and for carrying the model, and it stays through a restart.
  2. One off the line? Tap MANUAL and tick it. For a big move, spin it near with the slider, then STOP.
  3. Step it onto the line with ↺ 1° and ↺ 10° (they go the way Forward/Reverse is set).
  4. Tap SAVE HOME twice (the second tap confirms). The screen says “Home saved: …”.
  5. Tap AUTO: the arms go back to the line, so you can check. Back to the date (or Run, TIME MACHINE, Today) sends them to the date.

Clamping the arms on: tap TRAVEL MODE, wait for the servos to stop, then clamp each arm pointing straight away from the screen. That is its home: nothing to save. To test one, move it (Run, or MANUAL), then TRAVEL MODE again before clamping the next. Only an arm that ends up out of the row needs the steps above (SAVE HOME). The servo list shows FROM ROW for each arm. Calibration survives power-offs (the servo and tube gears are both 40-tooth, one turn for one turn); redo a planet only if its gear or collar slips.
