The 1/16, 3/32, 1/8 and 5/32 in tubes are spares. No sleeve: the steel rod goes straight inside Mercury's tube.

  • Check the factory end is square first. Wrap masking tape with its edge on the mark: the tape edge gives a square line all the way round.
  • Put the cutter wheel on the tape edge, press lightly, and tighten about ⅛ turn per revolution. The 0.014 in wall crushes easily if you go faster.
  • The cutter rolls a lip inward. Ream until the next smaller tube (still full length) slides through under its own weight, then sand the outside edge lightly.
  • Length within ±1/32 in (±1 mm); stands on the table without rocking; turns freely on the next tube all along its length. Flag it with tape and its planet's name.
  • The extra length just goes deeper into the Sun: about 3½ in of the 4 in ball, ½ in short of its top. Measure it: if it's longer than 16¼ in, trim it. Keep the other nine rods.

  • Clamp the gear flat and drill slowly with the exact-size bit from the 29-piece set. If a hole is tight, ease it with the tapered reamer from both faces. Aim for a snug slide, not a press fit: a tight gear squeezes the thin tube onto the one inside it. Keep the other 8 gears for phase 4.
  • As built (7 Nov 2026): Mercury's gear flush with its tube end. Every other gear set in the stack with calipers, 8.5 mm from the top of the gear below to its bottom face (Neptune 12.5 mm), the tube marked there and the gear glued on the bench. The gears are about 4 mm thick, so the levels came out ½ in apart (the design said 0.65 for 8 mm gears).
  • Roughen the tube under the gear with sandpaper. Put epoxy inside the hole and its moulded slots only, never on the gear's faces. Let it cure fully.
  • "Drill" means open the hole to that size: clamp the washer between two scraps of wood to drill it. If a washer is too tight to slide on, a twist of the reamer frees it.
  • Washers in between. Every tube should turn freely without dragging its neighbour. Tube tops step up 0.5 in each.
4
Servo gears and the first mesh testOne gear pair, before anything is fixed
  • 4 × M3 screws with washers, through the gear's own holes. You have about ±0.9 mm of play, so centre it by eye before tightening. Keep each servo with its planet label (1 Mercury … 8 Neptune).
  • Turn it one full revolution by hand. Feel for tight spots and watch for wobble.
  • Run that servo from the touchscreen. If the tube turns clockwise, the software's ARM_DIR needs flipping to −1: ask Claude to do it.
5
Base and centre columnThe two ¼ in sheets
Step by step, with the wobble check: stands.html walks through the post, the column, measuring each gear's wobble, a printable drilling template for the stand rods, and meshing one servo before the rest (phases 5 and 6 here).
Cutting acrylic: score along a clamped steel ruler with the scoring knife, many light passes (about 5–8 for ⅛ in, 10–15 for ¼ in), then snap it over a table edge with the score line facing up. Drilling: slow speed, light pressure, scrap wood clamped underneath.
  • Don't drill it yet. The offcut makes the 4 magnet blocks, and you can practise drilling on its very end first.
  • Choose which edge is the back. The column stands 6 in from either side and 3¾ in from the back edge (behind the middle, so the outer planets swing less far over the screen).
  • Wrap tape round the bit 3/16 in from its tip as a depth flag.
  • Check it with a square from two sides, at right angles to each other, while the epoxy sets.
  • Mercury's tube straight over the rod onto the collar, no washer: its flush gear's bottom face is 2 3/16 in above the base. A little play on the rod is fine; if it rattles, wrap a few layers of masking tape round the rod just above the collar and near Mercury's top. Then for each next planet: its washer onto the gear of the tube inside, then its tube. A drop of light oil between tubes, never grease.
  • To raise or lower the whole stack, loosen the collar's set screw and slide it.

  • For the material, score a 4 11/16 in strip off one ⅛ in sheet (the back-door sheet: the 7 5/16 × 12 in piece left is the door, already at its height; its length is trimmed to the opening once the box is glued), snap two 2 in strips off it (the 11/16 in left over is scrap), and cut each into 1⅞ in pieces: 12 plates, 8 + 4 spare. Printable template: stands page. Two 13/64 in holes 1⅜ in apart, 0.42 in in from the far-end edge, for the rods (4 Nov 2026: at 1.3 in the nut on top hit the servo's side; ease the rods apart about 1/32 in to drop the plate on). Snug, no slots: if the mesh needs the servo nearer or further, ease both holes that way. (25 Oct 2026: the plate goes under the whole servo; it was a ¼ in plate under its far end.)
  • Plug its cable(s) in first: the plugs lie flat under the servo. Set the servo on the plate, centred side to side, its far end (away from the gear) 5/32 in in from the plate's far edge. It rests on its end pad and round boss. Look up through the clear plate, dot its four bottom holes (Ø1.6 mm, a 0.96 × 0.81 in rectangle) and drill them 7/64 in. The holes sit about 1/16 in above the plate: stack M2 nylon washers on each screw between the plate and the servo to fill that. Four M2 × 10 self-tapping (pointed) screws up through the plate, snug, not hard: a machine screw won't start in them. No screw in the round boss.
  • Run a nut on before cutting; unscrewing it afterwards cleans up the cut thread.
  • For each servo in turn: hold it at its height with its gear meshed against its tube's gear (1.575 in between centres), and mark the two rod holes under the servo's outer end. A full-length post would hit the next servo's gear.
  • Each rod goes through the base, a nut on top and a washer + nut underneath. Thumb nut under the plate (one turn = 0.8 mm), a plain lock nut on top. Cables, 8½ in each, chained: driver board → Mercury → Venus → Earth → Mars → Jupiter → Saturn → Uranus → Neptune. Each next servo is 80° round clockwise (from above), so the cable to the next leaves a servo's right side (looking out from the column) and the one from before its left; Mercury's both leave on the right, toward the board. Each runs along its plate's side edge, outside the lock nut, off the plate's outer end and round the outside of the ring to the next plate, the slack (about 1½ in) bowed outward, away from the gears; a dab of tape on each plate. Build in 3D → Stand steps, step 8, shows it.
  • Drill a 1/8 in hole at each corner, 0.9 in in from both edges. Each foot on an M3 × 16 mm screw from the M3 kit: head down in the foot's recess (recess facing the table) with a 1 mm M3 nylon washer under the head, so it can't slip through the foot's own steel washer; up through the foot and the base; a nut on top. Snug, not squashed: a little thread shows above the nut (done 25 Oct 2026). They lift the nuts under the base clear of the table and quieten the servos.
  • Check the touchscreen's HEALTH tab shows every servo connected.

  • Screen on: measure the glass, the black border left and top of the picture, the picture itself, and the depth of the tray behind it. Prop the screen at 30–45° on the table and see which angle is easiest for your child to use (the plan uses 35°).
  • Acrylic scrap first, then one short cut near a corner of the spare polycarbonate sheet. Dial about 4 to 6. Cut along a clamped straightedge and measure from it to the nearest edge of the slot: that offset goes into the Box page, and the template then shows where the straightedge sits for each cut.
  • One sheet each. Across at 7.6 in up from the bottom edge, then the corner. Cut one, check it, trace it onto the second. The gap should be about 11.75 in: the front, the slope and the top are cut to what you measure.
  • Stick the printed template into a corner of the whole sheet, drill the four corner holes, make the four cuts in order. Then the slope strip (5.0 from the top edge), the front strip (3.5 from the bottom edge), and both to the measured gap.
    • Switch: step bit to ¾ in, then file to 20 mm.
    • Charging socket: step bit to 5/16 in, then file to 8 mm.
    • Right side: the converters' standoff holes, 1/8 in, marked from the converters.
    • Door finger hole: step bit to 1 in.
    • Top's column hole (the column's top bearing): step bit to 3/8 in, then the 13/32 twist bit slowly, then the tapered reamer from both faces until Neptune's tube turns freely with no rattle.
  • The 7/16 in collar goes on Neptune's tube first, then the top drops over the tube. Check every tube still turns freely with the top on, and that the screen fits its opening.
  • Test the cement on offcuts first, acrylic to acrylic and polycarbonate to acrylic; if the polycarbonate joint peels, use 5-minute epoxy for the front and the slope. Outdoors or at an open window. Hold each joint together, then run the thin cement into it from the needle applicator; it wicks in. The slope is glued by its two ends; no bevels. The top is only taped on, after phase 8: from the back door you can't reach the screen past the servo ring.
  • Cement the four blocks into the inside corners of the back opening, their back faces 0.36 in in from the back edge, so the door ends up flush. Epoxy (not cement) a magnet on each block, and one on the door's inside face opposite each. Test that each pair attracts before gluing.

Electronics into the boxAlready wired and tested on the battery (3–4 Oct)

  • Velcro it to the base, starting 1.4 in in from the back edge so the plug has room.
  • 7.5 V converter centred 2.8 in behind the middle, 0.3 in off the floor; the 5 V USB-C converter just in front of the middle.

Tilt it about the direction Saturn travels: one edge tips up toward the Sun, the other dips on the outside, and it stays level front to back.   • Each servo is parked at step 0 and you note which way its arm points, so the software can store an offset per planet (ARM_ZERO_DEG). Ask Claude to walk you through it.
